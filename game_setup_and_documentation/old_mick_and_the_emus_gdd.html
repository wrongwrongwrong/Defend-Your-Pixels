Old Mick and the Emus
One stubborn farmer. His useless son. A rusted fence. And about ten thousand emus who don't give a damn.

The Story

It is 1932. The Australian government has deployed soldiers to help Western Australian farmers deal with an emu invasion. The soldiers are losing. This is historically accurate.

Old Mick has farmed his patch of outback his entire life. He's not moving. He's got riflemen, a boundary fence, and forty years of stubbornness on his side. His goal is simple: destroy enough emu nests that the mob can't sustain itself and collapses. If he finds the Bird Council, it ends instantly.

On the other side of that wire: the emus. Hungry, relentless, and not interested in negotiation. The emus raid Old Mick's farms to steal grain — and that grain is everything. Raid enough farms and eventually something very old gets summoned. If they find the Grain Stash first, it ends there.

Somewhere on each side, hidden in the chaos, is the one thing that ends the game if it falls. Find theirs. Protect yours. The fence stopped meaning anything weeks ago.

The Board

A 12×12 grid split diagonally from top-right to bottom-left. Each square on the grid is a tile. Old Mick owns the outback paddock half — dry, exposed, hard-won. The emus own the scrublands half — dense, tangled, unknowable. The diagonal is the fence line: rusted wire, wooden posts, and absolutely no respect from either side.

Tiles that contain something worth fighting over — farms, nests, or terrain — are called cells. Farms are scattered across Old Mick's half at the start. Emu nests are scattered across the scrublands the same way. Lose enough and you can't fund anything. Somewhere on each half, hidden in the chaos, is the one thing that ends the game if it falls.

On your turn you direct your ATK token — pick a direction and it charges in a straight line, hits the first enemy cell it reaches across the fence, and returns. Horizontal, vertical, or diagonal. Unlimited range, but the line must be clean. Where you position your token determines what you can hit. That positioning is the game.

Tokens

Each side has three token types. Tokens cannot be destroyed — they act, they move, they project power. Only cells can be destroyed.

Emu Pack
ATK Token × 2 — Emu side
Two packs, two raids per turn. Each turn you direct an Emu Pack — it charges in a straight line, horizontal, vertical, or diagonal, hits the first farm it reaches across the fence, and returns. The more farms each Pack destroys, the more damage it deals per raid. Each token levels up independently.
Cassowary
DEF Token — Emu side
Doesn't raid. Just stands there and radiates menace. Projects a 3×3 perimeter zone around itself — anything inside that zone is shielded. Attackers must break through the perimeter before they can destroy a cell. Move the Cassowary and the perimeter fully resets. One per game. Irreplaceable.
The Riflemen
ATK Token × 2 — Farmer side
Two riflemen, two shots per turn. Each turn you direct a Rifleman — he fires in a straight line, horizontal, vertical, or diagonal, hits the first nest it reaches across the fence, and returns. The more nests each Rifleman destroys, the more damage he deals per shot. Each token levels up independently.
Old Mick
DEF Token — Farmer side
Not moving. Not negotiating. Projects a 3×3 perimeter zone around himself — anything inside is shielded. Attackers must break through the perimeter before they can destroy a cell. Move Old Mick and the perimeter fully resets. One per game. Irreplaceable.
Keith's Cannon
Wild Token — Farmer side
One use only. Unlocks when own cells reach critically low. Place the 3×3 zone anywhere in emu territory — 5 random cells destroyed instantly. Bypasses perimeter. You choose the zone. You don't choose the 5. Keith does not come back.
The Ancestors
Wild Token — Emu side
One use only. Unlocks when own cells reach critically low. Place the 3×3 zone anywhere in farmer territory — 5 random cells destroyed instantly. Bypasses perimeter. You choose the zone. You don't choose the 5. The Ancestors return to wherever they came from.

Movement & Combat

ATK movement Straight line only — horizontal, vertical, or diagonal. Unlimited range. Hits the first enemy cell it reaches across the fence line, then returns. That is one action.
DEF movement Same straight-line rules. Repositioning spends its action for that turn. Perimeter resets on move.
Target restriction ATK tokens can only destroy enemy cells. You cannot destroy your own cells.
Cell health Every cell has 1 health. One hit destroys it — unless it is inside the DEF token's perimeter zone.

DEF Token — Perimeter

The DEF token projects a perimeter zone around itself. Any cell inside that zone requires the perimeter to be broken on that tile first — then the cell can be destroyed on the next hit. The moment the DEF token moves, the entire perimeter resets around its new position.

The Wild Tokens

Each side has one Wild Token. It unlocks when your cells reach a dangerously low count — a last resort, not an opening move. Once used it is gone. It cannot be reused. It bypasses perimeter defences entirely — whatever it hits is destroyed instantly, no layers. The catch: you place the 3×3 zone but you don't choose which 5 cells inside it get destroyed. That's down to chance. Keith might take out the Bird Council. He might not. The Ancestors might hit the Grain Stash. They might miss. That's the deal.

Keith's Cannon
Old Mick's son. Nobody asks about Keith. When things get desperate enough, Old Mick opens the shed. Keith takes the ute, a cannon, and absolutely no plan. Place the zone anywhere in the scrublands. 5 cells destroyed at random within that 3×3. You don't pick which 5. Keith does not come back.
Farmer Wild Token — targets emu territory
Keith Crosses the Fence
Place the 3×3 zone anywhere in the scrublands. Keith charges through and destroys 5 randomly selected cells — nests, terrain, Bird Council, Burrow, whatever is there. Perimeter means nothing. One use only.
One use only — Keith is gone
The Ancestors
The emus have been here longer than anyone. They remember things. They call on things. When losses reach a breaking point, the ritual begins. Something erupts from the scrublands that has no business being in 1932. Place the zone. 5 cells gone at random. No explanation given. None needed.
Emu Wild Token — targets farmer territory
It Crosses the Fence
Place the 3×3 zone anywhere in Old Mick's paddock. The Ancestor charges through and destroys 5 randomly selected cells — farms, terrain, Grain Stash, Barn, whatever is there. Perimeter means nothing. One use only.
One use only — the Ancestors return to wherever they came from

Both Wild Tokens unlock when your losses reach a critical point — but you choose when to deploy. There is no forced timing. Place the zone, accept the randomness, live with the result.

Tone & Aesthetic Direction

Outback comedy with genuine menace underneath. The Emu War of 1932 is the lore — the Australian government deployed soldiers against emus, declared victory, and the emus won. The board looks like an aerial view of the Western Australian outback: red dirt on one side, dense scrub on the other, a rusted fence running through the middle. Damage animations scatter emu feathers and wheat chaff. Terrain tiles show visible wear as they take damage. Keith's Cannon plays a ute engine rev, a cannon blast, and then silence. The Ancestors plays nothing — just the sound of something very large moving through scrub.

Destroy the Grain Stash and Old Mick's screen fades to a single corrugated iron wall, one bullet hole through it. Destroy the Bird Council and the emu side goes quiet — a single cracked egg, dark scrublands. Find the Barn or the Burrow and it ends the same way — no fanfare, just silence and a result.
