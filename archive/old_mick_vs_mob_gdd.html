One stubborn farmer. His useless son. A rusted fence. And about ten thousand emus who don't give a damn.

The Story

It is 1932. The Australian government has deployed soldiers to help Western Australian farmers deal with an emu invasion. The soldiers are losing. This is historically accurate.

Old Mick has farmed his patch of outback his entire life. He's not moving. He's got riflemen, a boundary fence, and forty years of stubbornness on his side. His goal is simple: destroy enough emu feeding grounds that the mob can't sustain itself and collapses. If he finds the Nest, it ends instantly.

On the other side of that wire: the Mob. Hungry, relentless, and not interested in negotiation. The emus raid Old Mick's wheat paddocks to steal grain — and that grain is everything. It's how they eat, how they bribe the ostriches who've arrived from foreign lands, and how they fund the dark magic that keeps escalating this whole situation. Raid enough paddocks, steal enough grain, and eventually something very old gets summoned. If they find the Homestead first, it ends there.

Somewhere on each side, hidden in the chaos, is the one thing that ends the game if it falls. Find theirs. Protect yours. The fence stopped meaning anything weeks ago.

The Board

A 12×12 grid split diagonally from top-right to bottom-left. Old Mick owns the outback paddock half — dry, exposed, hard-won. The Mob owns the scrublands half — dense, tangled, unknowable. The diagonal is the fence line: rusted wire, wooden posts, and absolutely no respect from either side.

Wheat paddocks are scattered across Old Mick's half at the start — weighted toward his corner but never neat. Emu feeding grounds are scattered across the scrublands the same way. Each one you hold generates resources every turn. Lose enough and you can't fund anything. Somewhere on each half, hidden in the chaos, is the one thing that ends the game if it falls.

On your turn you direct your ATK token — pick a direction and it charges in a straight line, hits the first enemy cell it reaches across the fence, and returns. Horizontal, vertical, or diagonal. Unlimited range, but the line must be clean. Where you position your token determines what you can hit. That positioning is the game.

Tokens & Terrain
The Mob
Attack token × 2 — Emu side
Each turn you direct a Mob token — it charges in a straight line, horizontal, vertical, or diagonal, hits the first wheat paddock it reaches across the fence, and returns. Two mob tokens, two raids per turn. Position them to open up the lines you need.
Cassowary
Defense token — Emu side
Doesn't raid. Doesn't roam. Just stands there and radiates menace. Every feeding ground within its 3×3 patrol zone gains +1 HP — takes 2 hits to destroy instead of 1. After Tier 2 upgrade that zone expands to 5×5. One cassowary per game. It is irreplaceable and it knows it.
The Riflemen
Attack token × 2 — Farmer side
Each turn you direct a Riflemen token — they fire in a straight line, horizontal, vertical, or diagonal, hit the first feeding ground they reach across the fence, and pull back. Two units, two shots per turn. A bullet doesn't curve. Position them to find the shot.
Old Mick
Defense token — Farmer side
The man himself. Planted on his land, rifle in hand. Every wheat paddock within his 3×3 patrol zone gains +1 HP — takes 2 hits to destroy instead of 1. After Tier 2 upgrade he gets a machine gun and that zone expands to 5×5. One Old Mick per game. He is irreplaceable and he knows it.
Fence Barricade
Hard terrain — Farmer side
Reinforced wire, iron sheets, whatever Old Mick could find. Impassable. Permanent. The emus go around it or they don't go at all.
Wheat Scarecrow
Soft terrain — Farmer side
Old Mick's last line of defence — a stick, a shirt, and a hat. Slows the mob down. Takes 2 hits to destroy. The emus always double-check it's not real. It never is. It doesn't matter.
Termite Mounds
Hard terrain — Emu side
Harder than concrete, taller than a man, packed in dense clusters. Impassable by anything. The army couldn't pursue emus into termite country. Neither can you.
Spinifex
Soft terrain — Emu side
Thick, spiky ground cover that tears through anything moving fast. Slows any token passing through it. Takes 2 hits to clear. The farmers hate it. The emus don't notice.
The Nest
Hidden HQ — Emu side
Hidden in the scrublands at the start. If the Riflemen find it and destroy it, the game ends. Every egg in the next generation, gone.
The Homestead
Hidden HQ — Farmer side
Old Mick's home. Hidden at the start. The Mob finds it, the farm is over. The old man has nowhere left to stand.

Upgrade Progression

Emus fund upgrades by raiding wheat paddocks — stolen grain pays the ostriches, and the ostriches bring the magic. Farmers fund upgrades by destroying feeding grounds — fewer emus means fewer raids, buys time, unlocks heavier firepower. Destruction fuels both sides equally.

Tone & Aesthetic Direction

Outback comedy with genuine menace underneath. The Emu War of 1932 is the lore — the Australian government deployed soldiers against emus, declared victory, and the emus won. The board looks like an aerial view of the Western Australian outback: red dirt on one side, dense scrub on the other, a rusted fence running through the middle. Damage animations scatter emu feathers and wheat chaff. Keith's nuke plays a ute engine rev, a cannon blast, and then silence. The Ancestors plays nothing — just the sound of something very large moving through scrub.

Destroy the Homestead and Old Mick's screen fades to a single corrugated iron wall, one bullet hole through it. Destroy the Nest and the emu side goes quiet — a single cracked egg, dark scrublands.
